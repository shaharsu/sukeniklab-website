Recently, the ability of proteins to form condensed phases has been shown to be critical
for various crucial cellular functions. This tendency for phase separation is modulated by
a delicate balance between protein structure and the cellular environment, altering the
physical and chemical properties of the condensed phase, and the behavior of the proteins
that must function within these phases. We look at how changes in the surrounding solution
alter the ability of proteins to form specific, functional, and stable condensates.
